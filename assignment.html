Task 1: Slate Health Check

1a Data quality audit and cleaning decisions

Table 1 lists the issues that had to be resolved first. Zero is used throughout the file as a stand-in for “unknown”: 1,037 films (21.6%) record a budget of exactly zero, 35 a positive budget below the USD 10,000 floor, and 1,427 zero revenue, while roi is missing on exactly those same zero-budget rows (TRUE). Identifiers are otherwise clean, with 0 duplicate IDs and only 3 repeated titles. Budgets below USD 10,000 and revenue of zero are therefore recoded as missing, and ROI is recomputed as revenue_c / budget_c rather than read from the file’s own column. That missingness is not random: budget coverage is 46.1% in Documentary against 90.6% in Adventure, 89.6% for franchises against 77.0% for originals, and falls from 92.6% in the 1950s to 75.3% in the 2000s. After cleaning, 3,731 films (77.7%) have a usable budget and 3,214 (66.9%) a usable ROI.

1b Overall distribution of budget and ROI

Panel A of Figure 1 shows raw budget heavily skewed right: median budget is USD 24.0 million versus a mean of USD 37.4 million and a maximum of USD 380 million. On a log-10 scale (Panel B), the distribution is approximately normal. Panel C shows ROI: median ROI is 2.30, with IQR spanning 1.03 to 4.42. About 24.4% of films sit below 1x ROI.

1c Slate composition by genre and franchise status

Horror is the cheapest focus genre (median USD 10 m) and most lucrative (median ROI 3.72, 87.8% recouped). Animation is expensive (median USD 75 m) but reliable (81.8% recouped). Drama performs weakest (median ROI 2.16). Franchise films constitute 5.4% of titles (Figure 3). Among complete cases (Table 3), franchise titles take 11.0% of budget while yielding 14.2% of revenue, outperforming originals on recoupment rate (94.1% vs 74.2%).

1d Trends over time and summary for the Head of Production

Decadal figures show median budgets peaking at USD 28 million in the 1990s/2000s and stabilizing since. However, median ROI declined steadily from a peak of 7.93 in the 1970s to 1.97 in the 2000s (Figure 4).

Summary for the Head of Production

Data Completeness: Only 77.7% of films have reliable budgets and 66.9% have reliable ROI. Unreported figures disproportionately occur in low-performing genres (Comedies/Dramas) and older eras, meaning raw financial averages slightly overstate true performance. Skewness & Metrics: Budget and ROI are heavily right-skewed (24.4% fail to reach 1x ROI). Medians and log-scales must guide strategic decisions rather than means. Portfolio Strategy: Horror provides low capital risk and top ROI (87.8% recouped). Animation warrants big budgets due to high reliability (81.8% recouped). Action carries high risk (25% fail to recover costs), while Drama shows consistently low returns. Franchises deliver superior returns (94.1% recoupment) and represent high-value investments. Temporal Risk: Modern slate greenlighting faces a tighter margin environment than historical periods. Greenlighting priority should be given to low-budget horror and scalable franchise concepts over mid-budget standalone dramas.
